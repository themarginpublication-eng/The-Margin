Section 01

Foundations.

The Margin exists to teach. We help the everyday Christian grow in biblical literacy, learning to read Scripture with depth and confidence, like a reader's own pen in the side of the page. Ancient depth, modern doorway.

Why we exist

To teach. We help the everyday Christian grow in biblical literacy, equipped to read Scripture with depth, confidence, and a pen in hand.

Audience

The everyday Christian, and the curious seeker, who wants to engage Scripture deeply but has no appetite for dry academic theology. People who want to be taught, not performed at. The reader profile sits next to John Mark Comer, The Bible Project, and Practicing the Way, hungry to grow, hospitable, allergic to hype.

Thesis

Biblical literacy is taught the way it's actually learned, slowly, carefully, one passage at a time, like a teacher writing notes in the margin of your Bible. We don't just explain the text; we hand readers the tools to read it themselves. Margin notes that teach you to read.

Voice · 01

Slow, but never soft.

Careful and patient, and unafraid to make the call.

Voice · 02

Ancient depth, said straight.

Old truth, plain words. We don't water Scripture down.

Voice · 03

Teach, never talk down.

Hand over the tools, not just the takeaway, so the reader can open the text and read it for themselves next time.

Voice · 04

Sure about Jesus.

Confident at the center. Honest about mystery at the edges.

Section 04

Color.

Seven colors. Quiet, warm, paper-based. Rust is the only color that ever raises its voice, and only by a half-step.

Parchment
#F3EEE3
Primary background. The page.
Vellum
#FAF6EC
Lighter surface, cards, alternates.
Margin
#C9BFAE
Rules, dividers, soft borders.
Rust
#A8593C
The accent. The rule. Links.
Gilt
#B68A47
Special moments. Used rarely.
Ash
#6B5F54
Muted secondary text.
Ink
#1A1814
Primary text. The hand.

"Rust is the only color that ever shouts. Everything else stays quiet."

Default ratio

70 parchment / 22 ink / 5 ash / 2 rust / 1 gilt. Hold this ratio across compositions, when in doubt, more paper.

Don't

Never let rust and gilt share equal weight. Never use more than one accent color in a single composition.

Section 05

Typography.

Three families. All three are free on Google Fonts and shipped inside Canva. Fraunces strikes; Newsreader 500 italic carries the soul voice with presence; Inter handles metadata. Nothing else.

Display
Fraunces 900

The bold strike. Wordmark, post titles, hero headlines. Used sparingly so it keeps its force.

On reading Paul slowly.
Soul voice
Newsreader 500

Editorial italic with presence. Pull quotes, taglines, deck copy, episode titles, social cards. 500 is the floor, never thinner. Reads on a phone.

The first readers heard this as a letter, in one sitting, with neighbors leaning in.
Body
Newsreader 400

The reader's chair. Anywhere the reader will read more than a sentence, 17–19 px, line-height 1.7.

Romans was not first opened as a textbook. It was a letter read aloud in a room, to a small house church, with people leaning forward to catch every word. We tend to read it in two-minute bursts on a phone. The Margin is an invitation to slow down, to underline, to ask what these sentences would have sounded like to the people who heard them first.

Emphasis
Newsreader 700

Author names, section headers within an article, occasional bolded phrases. Less than you think.

Paul writes to a church he has never met, and yet he calls them family before he is two sentences in.

Labels
INTER 500 CAPS

Labels, dates, navigation, metadata. Always caps, 0.18–0.28 letter-spacing. Should feel almost invisible.

Section 06

Boundaries.

The brand is held together more by what we refuse than what we add. Every boundary serves the same end: a page clear enough to teach from. If a choice feels louder, smaller, or busier, it's almost certainly not us.

Do

Use the rust vertical rule wherever a divider naturally lives. It is the signature.

Don't

Don't use more than one accent color in a single composition. Rust or Gilt, not both.

Do

Embrace breathing room and white space. The margin is part of the design, not what's left over.

Don't

Don't fill a composition edge-to-edge. Always leave room for the reader's eye to rest.

Do

Keep type quiet except for one headline moment per piece, one strike of Fraunces 900.

Don't

Don't use a sans-serif as the primary typeface. It breaks the editorial feel. Inter is metadata only.

Do

Use sentence case for headlines and body. LETTER-SPACED CAPS only for metadata labels.

Don't

Don't use exclamation marks, hype copy, or church-marketing language, no "epic," "level up," "game-changing."

Do

Pair Fraunces with Newsreader only. The system is two voices, never a random third.

Don't

Don't use gradients, drop shadows, glows, or busy textures. Paper, ink, and ratio do the work.

Do

Treat Scripture with reverence, accurate references, careful framing, paraphrase if a translation is copyrighted.

Don't

Don't sound like you have all the answers. We teach to equip, not to impress, margin notes, not lectures.

Section 08

Voice in use.

Slow does not mean invisible. The voice teaches, it catches attention with specificity and noticing, never with hype, and always leaves the reader more able to read Scripture themselves. The "not this / this" pairs below are the brand's working rule.

Principle · 01

Specific, never vague.

Name the book, the chapter, the sentence. Vagueness is the cousin of hype.

Principle · 02

Conviction, not hype.

Say it with a spine, not with capital letters. Truth doesn't need exclamation marks.

Principle · 03

Invite, then call.

Open with a noticing or a question, then ask the reader to actually respond.

Instagram caption · essay launch

Lead with the observation, name the book, end with the invitation.

Not this

"🔥 This Romans deep-dive is going to TRANSFORM your faith. Don't miss out, link in bio!!"

This

You weren't built to skim the Bible. Romans is a letter before it's a lecture, slow down, learn to read it, and let it ask something of you. New essay, link in bio.

Article headline

A noticing or an admission. Never a number. Never "you'll never believe."

Not this

"5 Mind-Blowing Truths About Romans That Will Transform Your Walk"

This

You've been reading Paul too fast, and it's costing you.

Reel · cover text

Five words at most. A single small instruction, or a single small noticing.

Not this

"MIND. BLOWN. Romans 1 will NEVER be the same 🤯"

This

Stop skimming. Read Romans 1 like it's true, sentence by sentence.

Quote card · pull quote

A line that earns its own card. Concrete image, no abstractions.

Not this

"Faith is the FUEL for your most authentic life. ✨"

This

The first readers heard Romans in one sitting, and then they had to decide what to do about it. So do we.

LinkedIn · post hook

First line is the noticing. Second line is the consequence. Then the link.

Not this

"5 leadership lessons hidden in Romans 12, you won't believe #3."

This

Before Romans is theology, it is a letter.
And a letter is written to be answered, not just admired.
A new essay on learning to read it well →

Call to action

A direction, not a demand. Lower-case verb, simple arrow.

Not this

"SUBSCRIBE NOW! Don't miss out, join the movement today!"

This

Read it. Then go live it. A new essay every other Sunday →

Bio · one-liner

What the work is, and who it's for. Nothing about you.

Not this

"Helping you UNLOCK the Bible 🔓 and live your most authentic faith life ever."

This

Margin notes for people who actually want to read, and follow, the Book they love.

Substack · publication description

A sentence about the work; a sentence about the reader.

Not this

"The #1 Christian newsletter for transforming your faith journey!"

This

Margin notes on the books of the Bible, teaching the everyday Christian to read Scripture with depth and confidence. Slow, careful commentary for people who'd rather spend time in the text than scrolling about it.

Words we use

Concrete verbs and nouns. Words a careful reader might underline.

slow· careful· notice· again· letter· reader· listen· underline· sentence· paragraph· neighbor· attention· invitation· mystery· first· before
Words we don't

Inflated verbs, marketing nouns, and church-platform jargon.

epic· game-changing· level up· unlock· crush it· authentic· journey· movement· community· brand· transform· mind-blowing
Six lines in the pocket

When stuck for a caption, start here.

"You weren't made to skim this."

"Before Romans is theology, it is a letter, and it's asking something of you."

"Slow down. Then don't look away."

"Read it like it's true, because it is."

"Don't just underline it. Live it."

"Learn to read it, and you won't need us to."

Section 09

In practice.

How to actually build this in the tools you use every day. The brand is a small set of decisions you make once in Canva, Google Slides, and Google Docs, then never again.

Canva

Brand Kit setup.

A one-time configuration that makes every new design land on-brand by default.

Step 01 · Brand colors

In Brand Kit → Colors, add all seven hex codes from Section 04. Label them by name (Parchment, Vellum, Margin, Rust, Gilt, Ash, Ink). Pin Parchment as the background default and Rust as the single accent.

Step 02 · Brand fonts

All three families are free on Canva. Set defaults: Headings, Fraunces, weight 900. Subheadings, Newsreader, 500 italic. Body, Newsreader, regular 400. Labels, Inter, 500, letter-space 220.

Step 03 · The margin rule

Use the rectangle tool. Set width to 2 px (3 px for hero canvases over 2000 px), height to the relevant content height, color to Rust. Group it with the wordmark and save as a Brand Element. Never use the line tool, its caps are inconsistent.

Step 04 · Templates folder

Create a folder called "The Margin · Templates" in Projects. Save each canvas from Section 07 as its own template. When publishing, duplicate first, edit second, never edit the master.

Step 05 · Effects off

Turn off Canva's default Effects panel before you start. No shadows, no glows, no curved text, no photo filters. The system does not need them.

Google Slides

Theme & master.

For teaching decks, sermons, and any 16:9 presentation.

Step 01 · Custom theme

View → Theme builder. Set the master slide background to Parchment (#F3EEE3). Add a 2 px Rust rectangle at the left edge with the wordmark in Fraunces 900 beside it, this is your default slide header.

Step 02 · Add the fonts

In any text box, open the font picker and click "More fonts." Search and add Fraunces, Newsreader, and Inter. They'll save into your account; this is a one-time step.

Step 03 · Four slide layouts

Build these as layouts under your theme: Title slide (Fraunces 900, 72 pt, centered), Scripture slide (rust caps label above verse text in Newsreader 500 italic), Main point slide (Fraunces 900, 48 pt, left-aligned), Section divider (Ink background, parchment wordmark only).

Step 04 · Spacing rule

Every slide gets a 10% margin on all four sides, minimum. Never fill the slide to the edge, the breathing room is the brand.

Google Docs

Manuscript style.

For drafts, sermon notes, and printable handouts.

Step 01 · Page setup

Page Color: Parchment (#F3EEE3). Margins: 1.25 in on all sides. Single column. Add a 2 px Rust line on the left margin if you want the signature on the page.

Step 02 · Paragraph styles

Title, Fraunces 900, 36 pt, Ink. Heading 1, Fraunces 900, 22 pt. Heading 2, Inter 500, 11 pt, all caps, letter-spaced (use Format → Text → Capitalization). Normal text, Newsreader 400, 12 pt, line spacing 1.5.

Step 03 · Pull quotes

Indent 0.5 in. Set to Newsreader 500 italic, 14 pt. Add a Rust left border (Format → Paragraph styles → Borders & shading → Left border, 2 pt, Rust).

Step 04 · Save as default

Format → Paragraph styles → Options → Save as my default styles. Now every new doc opens on-brand.

Substack

Publication settings.

The few places Substack lets you control the brand, get these right and the rest mostly follows.

Logo upload (square)

256 × 256 px. Wordmark stacked: "the / margin" with the rule. Ink on Parchment, exported as transparent PNG.

Wordmark upload (horizontal)

1344 × 256 px. Wordmark + rule + tagline lockup, left-aligned with 6% padding. Use the template in Section 07.

Accent color

Set Substack's accent to Rust (#A8593C). This colors buttons, links, and the highlight bar in one stroke.

Cover photos

Use the Article Hero template (1600 × 900). Never use Substack's stock photos. If you don't have an image for the post, use the typographic hero, title in Fraunces, deck in Newsreader italic.

Publication description

Use the line from Section 08, "Margin notes on the books of the Bible, teaching the everyday Christian to read Scripture with depth and confidence." Don't expand it.

Section 10

Checklist.

Before anything goes out, a post, a slide, a header, a cover, run it past this list. If a row fails, fix it before publishing.

Before you publish
01

Is the margin rule present? A thin rust line beside the wordmark, the byline, or the quote, somewhere.

02

Only one accent color? Rust or Gilt, never both. No third accent ever.

03

Only one display moment? One strike of Fraunces 900 per composition. Everything else is body or label.

04

Breathing room on all four sides? Minimum 8–10% margin. If it feels cramped, shrink the text.

05

Wordmark in lockup, lowercase, with rule? Never set the wordmark alone. Never typed in caps. Never re-tracked.

06

Metadata in Inter caps? Dates, episode numbers, page labels, all 500 weight, letter-spaced 0.22 em or more.

Before you press send
07

No hype words? No "epic," "transform," "unlock," "level up," "movement," or "journey." No exclamation marks.

08

Did I notice, not conclude? The hook is an observation or a question, not a takeaway or a tip.

09

Specific reference? Book, chapter, verse, named. No floating "scripture says" without a citation.

10

Scripture quoted carefully? Translation noted, or paraphrased honestly. Never a copyrighted full-screen image quote.

11

Does it read on a phone? Body text 16 px+. Headlines hold at 480 px wide. No type smaller than 10 px on social.

12

Does it sound like an invitation? Read it aloud. If it points at me instead of up, rewrite it.

A closing note

Teach it slow. Teach it true. And leave the reader more able to open the Book themselves, pointed up, not at us.

One quiet system.
Held lightly.

This book is a reference, not a referee. When a piece of work feels off, the answer is almost always: slow it down, take something out, and let the margin do its job, teaching the reader to see what's already on the page.
