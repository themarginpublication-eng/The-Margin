03What to launch with

Choose it slowly. Choose it yours.

The first series defines what The Margin is — so don't rush it. Live in the text through the summer and let one direction start to feel like yours. The one rule: don't open in someone else's lane. "How to read the Bible" is The Bible Project's territory — lead there and you look like a follower. Open instead by doing a close reading so good it proves the whole premise: you've been reading this too fast.

Lane 01 · A book people overlook

The overlooked book

Ecclesiastes — brutally honest; for the seeker. Ruth — a four-chapter novella. Esther — the book where God is never named. Philemon — a single radical page. Habakkuk — a prophet who argues with God.

Lane 02 · A thread you trace

The thread across Scripture

The questions God asks. The questions Jesus asks. The prayers of the Bible. Sabbath & rest. Covenant — the spine of the whole story. One idea, followed Genesis to Revelation.

Lane 03 · A person under-taught

The figure we skip

Tamar (Genesis 38) — wronged, and in Jesus' line. The women of Matthew 1 — five scandalous, foreign, unexpected names. Hagar — the first person to name God. Jacob — the deceiver, read fresh.

Lane 04 · A passage you dwell in

One text, read for weeks

Psalm 23 — slowed until it stops being a funeral cliché. 1 Corinthians 13 — rescued from weddings, read as a knife. John 1 — the opening hymn you already live in daily.

Notice which lane you keep returning to over the summer. That's the tell. When one feels like yours, we build the full arc around it.

13The repurposing workflow

One essay → a week of presence.

The essay is the only heavy lift. Everything else is cutting, not creating. Batch it in one sitting — about an hour beyond the essay buys a full week.

The batch — ~60 min after the essay
1Pull the key passage → annotated carousel (~20 min)
2Pull your 3 best lines → 3 Threads/X notes (~10 min)
3Pull the thesis → LinkedIn reflection (~15 min, biweekly)
4Write the Facebook intro + link (~5 min)
5Draft one reader question for Thursday (~5 min)
6Schedule it all, incl. Wednesday's note (~5 min)
The rules of the engine
Write the note once — post to Threads and X both.
Schedule, don't hover. Tools: Buffer, Meta Planner, or native scheduling.
A backlog of essays means a backlog of posts — October coasts on this.
Every post ends pointing home to the hub.
If a week is brutal, drop to Threads notes only. Never go fully silent without it being a choice.

17The annotated passage

Made by hand, on your iPad.

The annotated passage is the post that looks like the brand — a reader's own pen in the margin. The trick is to split it: let Canva hold the consistency, let Procreate add the soul. Neither tool alone does both well.

Why two tools
CanvaHolds the fixed brand frame — parchment, wordmark, rust rule, the typeset passage. Same every time, no rebuilding.
ProcreateAdds the hand — real pen pressure, underlines, circled words, arrows, a margin note in your handwriting. This is the warmth Canva can't fake.
The hand-on-template approach is exactly how the "cool annotated" accounts actually do it.
The workflow · ~10 min a post
1Open the Canva template; type in the passage + citation.
2Export it as a PNG (1080×1080).
3In Procreate, drop the PNG as the bottom layer.
4On a new layer, annotate by hand — rust brush + ink brush only.
5Flatten & export. Post.
Your iPad toolkit
Procreate ($12, one-time) — the right tool. Not the subscription apps.
An Apple Pencil — pressure sensitivity is the whole point.
Two brushes only: a dry-ink pen (Procreate's "Technical Pen" or "Syrup") for marks, and a soft pencil for margin notes.
A saved rust + ink color palette so every mark is on-brand.
What I'll build you (when ready)
1A Canva annotated-passage template — the parchment frame, wordmark, rule, and a clear "annotation zone."
2A Procreate color-swatch file (rust, ink, gilt) you import once.
3A one-page brush + settings cheat-sheet so it's identical every time.
Just say the word and I'll make all three in the brand.

Start simple: one underline in rust + one margin note in your hand is already unmistakably The Margin. Restraint reads as craft.
