Content Studio

Take a seed — a passage, a person, a book, an idea, a word — through a six-move method into a finished, publishable series. The method is a default: turn moves off, rename them, or leave it behind in Free mode. Nothing here publishes; a finished series hands off to Series and Daily notes.

Method: Study System · Frames: Template library

Free mode

Your own way.

No moves, no gates, no question types. A title, a place to think, and as many days as you want. Nothing here is validated and nothing is required — switch back to the guided method any time from Method settings, and this draft stays where it is.

Days

Add as many as the material wants.

Why this exists

A method you cannot leave is a cage.

Some series arrive whole and the six moves would only slow you down. Free mode keeps the workspace, the frames and the export, and drops everything else. Your guided series is untouched underneath.

Setup

Method settings.

The six moves are a default, not a rule. Turn off what you don't use, rename anything to your own language, reorder them, or leave the method behind entirely.

Mode

What turning a move off does

Nothing is deleted.

The move disappears from the tab bar, stops counting toward progress, and its readiness checks stop firing. Anything you already wrote in it stays in the workspace and comes back if you turn it on again.

Dependencies

Two moves feed others.

Move 02 supplies the observations move 05 deals out, and move 04 sets the day count moves 05 and 06 use. Turn either off and the downstream move falls back to free entry rather than breaking.

Readiness

Optional too.

Move 01

Cut the unit before you study it.

Fix the boundaries first. Six markers signal a break; where translations disagree, you have found a seam worth a post. For a person, an idea or a word, the cut comes from the seed's own logic instead.

There is no unified standard for pericope division, so this field is evidence, not pedantry.

Move 02

Observe until you have too much.

Only what is there — repetitions, odd words, who speaks and who does not, what is absent, what the narrator knows that no character could. Tag each one with the question it answers; that tag is what deals it onto a day later.

Tagging rules

Every observation answers exactly one question. Untagged observations cannot be dealt onto a day.

Meaning

Shows the reader something they walked past, then makes it legible — a word, a structure, a piece of background law.

Truth

Tests the claim. Where else does Scripture do this? What is the strongest objection?

Difference

Names what changes — first in the text, then for the reader. Concrete, never "trust God more."

Move 03

State the idea as one sentence.

Subject plus complement: what are you talking about, and what are you saying about it? If the sentence will not come, you have not studied enough — that is a diagnostic, not a writing problem. Then write it again for your reader.

Exegetical idea — what the text says

These are not the same sentence

And they are not supposed to be.

The exegetical idea is accountable to the text. The homiletical idea is accountable to the person reading it on a phone at 7am. Robinson's whole point is that the road between them is real work, and that the second one is your series title.

The cap

Sixteen words. A message should be a bullet, not buckshot.

Move 04

Write the last day first.

What can the reader do on their own afterwards that they could not do before? Name a capability, not a feeling. The day count is then whatever it takes to get there — and the question-type plan falls out automatically.

A verb they perform on a text you never covered. "Appreciate Ruth" is not a capability.
Derived plan

Why the order is fixed

Backward design, applied to a reader rather than a student.

Meaning days carry the research and go first. Truth days test the claim and sit in the middle. Difference days name what changes and close. Longer series do not add new kinds of day — they hold one kind open longer.

Ten and fourteen

Ten runs the whole sequence twice over two halves of one seed. Fourteen runs it twice over two seeds that share one skill.

Move 05

Deal the days by question type.

Each day takes exactly one observation, and only observations tagged with that day's question can fill it. This is the answer to "what goes on day three" — day three is whichever truth-tagged observation is strongest.

What is left over

Spare observations are not waste. They are the next series, or the objection day when this one runs to seven.

Move 06

Shape each day in four moves.

Hook, Book, Look, Took — the four-move lesson shape, which is also a four-slide carousel. The observation the day carries is fixed; these four fields turn it into something publishable.

One instruction. Never "share this."

Preview

Why four

Larry Richards, Creative Bible Teaching.

The four moves take a reader from head knowledge, through the implications, to something practised. A day missing Took is information. A day missing Hook is a lecture.

Before publishing

Readiness.

Computed from the workspace, not self-reported. Two of these come straight out of the literature's known failure modes.

Score

—

A failing check is not a blocker. It is a note about which move you left early.
